# BSE Limited — Company Note

**Ticker:** BSE | **Price (29 Oct 2026):** ₹3,502 | **Market cap:** ₹1,42,846 crore | **EV:** ₹1,40,346 crore

## Business

BSE is Asia's oldest exchange and India's #2 equity exchange by cash volume, with a growing derivatives franchise. Revenue comes from transaction charges, listing fees, co-location/data, the StAR MF platform and investment income.

FY26 normalized revenue was ₹5,124 crore and PAT ₹2,487 crore. BSE's own media release reports total revenue of ₹5,148 crore (₹4,834 crore from operations plus ₹314 crore investment/other income) and operating EBITDA of ₹3,079 crore; the normalized line reclassifies ~₹290 crore of investment/other income into revenue, so its 67.9% margin is not the company's 64% operating margin. Transaction charges were ₹3,795 crore, about 74% of revenue; in Q1 FY27 they were ₹1,328 crore, about 85% of the ₹1,566 crore quarterly revenue, showing increasing derivatives dependence. Q1 FY27 revenue grew 30% YoY and PAT 44% YoY to ₹873 crore.

## Moat

- **Liquidity network:** BSE had 55.4% notional derivatives market share in April 2026 and about 34% options-premium share. Liquidity is self-reinforcing for traders and market makers.
- **Regulatory licence and index franchise:** Sensex/Bankex products and exchange status are hard to replicate.
- **Operating leverage:** 67.9% normalized FY26 EBITDA margin (64% on BSE's reported operating revenue); incremental revenue drops through at very high margins.
- **But:** the moat is contested by NSE, and the fastest-growing revenue line is the most exposed to SEBI policy. This is a strong but regulatory-beta moat, not a stable consumer-style moat.

## Growth

- Derivatives volume/share gains, particularly expiry-day index options.
- StAR MF and listing/data diversification, but still small relative to transaction charges.
- Q1 FY27 momentum is strong; FY27E revenue +20% and EPS ₹72.7 in the base case.
- Growth is high but mean-reverting risk is high: SEBI's August 2026 study highlighted expiry-day concentration and retail losses.

## Financial quality

- FY26 CFO ₹3,104 crore, FCF ₹2,589 crore, FCF/EBITDA 74%; net cash.
- Average-equity ROE 44.8%; pre-tax ROCE 49.8%.
- FCF yield only 1.8% at the current market cap; P/FCF 55.2x.
- Cash balance includes clearing/SGF-related balances; the model uses a ₹2,500 crore attributable net-cash haircut.

## Thesis

**Bull:** Derivatives share gains continue, operating leverage drives 20%+ EPS growth, and the market keeps a 40–50x multiple. StAR/listing/data become a second leg.

**Bear:** SEBI tightens expiry-day rules or position limits, derivatives volume/price realization falls, and the stock de-rates from 50x to 30x. A 30% derivatives turnover fall alone cuts FY27 EPS by ~28%; the joint adverse case with a 20% equity drawdown cuts EPS by ~35% and the target by ~51% versus the base target.

**Falsifiers:**
- BSE derivatives notional/premium share falls below 45%/25% for two quarters.
- SEBI imposes a further structural restriction on weekly expiries or contract size.
- Transaction-charge realization falls >10% YoY while volume growth is <10%.

## Verdict

**Reject.** The growth story is real, but the risk asymmetry is poor: the stock is priced at 50x TTM with a 40x EV/EBITDA multiple, while its main profit pool is the most regulated part of the value chain. The base case is −0.9% and the bear case is −52%; the probability-weighted return is −5.0%. BSE is the high-beta alternative, not the risk-adjusted winner.
