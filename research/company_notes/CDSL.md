# Central Depository Services (India) Limited — Company Note

**Ticker:** CDSL | **Price (29 Oct 2026):** ₹1,282 | **Market cap:** ₹26,794 crore | **EV:** ₹25,096 crore

## Business

CDSL is one of India's two depositories. It earns from annual issuer charges, depository transaction fees, KYC/AML services through CVL, corporate actions, e-voting and related services. Revenue is regulated and recurring, with issuer charges the largest block (~40% of revenue), followed by transaction and KYC charges.

Operating metrics are strong: 18.59 crore demat accounts at 30 June 2026 (~80% market share), AUC ₹88.2 lakh crore, 58 lakh new accounts in Q1 FY27, and 81.4% incremental share in June 2026 (down 420 bps from March 2026).

## Moat

- **Two-sided network:** depository participants, issuers and investors all benefit from CDSL's scale and reach.
- **Switching costs:** changing a depository is operationally cumbersome for DPs and issuers.
- **Regulatory licence:** only two depositories exist; new entry is effectively barred.
- **But:** pricing is visible and regulated, and NSDL competes for issuer/institutional business. The moat is wide, but it does not guarantee high growth.

## Growth

- Demat account additions and AUC growth; retail participation remains the main driver.
- KYC and adjacent services.
- Growth is maturing: FY26 revenue grew 5.8% and PAT fell 13.5% as costs/depreciation rose; Q1 FY27 revenue grew 13.1% and PAT 15.7% YoY, but the quarterly PAT is still below the FY25 peak.
- Base case: FY27 revenue +9%, EPS ₹23.3; FY28 EPS ₹25.9.

## Financial quality

- FY26 CFO ₹467 crore, FCF ₹346 crore, FCF/EBITDA 59%; net cash.
- Average-equity ROE 25.1%; pre-tax ROCE 26.3%.
- P/FCF 77.4x and FCF yield 1.3% — the weakest cash-flow valuation support in the universe.
- Investments include regulatory/operating balances; the model applies a net-cash haircut.

## Thesis

**Bull:** Demat additions re-accelerate, CDSL defends its retail share, KYC/adjacent services scale, and the market continues to pay >50x for a regulated network.

**Bear:** Account growth slows, issuer/transaction fees face regulatory scrutiny, and the multiple compresses toward 35x. A 30% derivatives turnover fall has only a ~3% revenue impact, but a 20% equity drawdown cuts revenue ~6% and EPS ~7.5%; the bigger risk is de-rating.

**Falsifiers:**
- Demat account additions fall below 30 lakh/quarter for two quarters.
- Incremental market share falls below 65%.
- SEBI/regulator mandates a material cut in issuer or transaction charges.
- FY27 EPS growth is negative despite positive AUM growth.

## Verdict

**Reject.** CDSL has the widest moat in the universe, but the market already pays for it: 57x TTM, 55x FY27E, 77x FCF, and a 5.7x PEG. The base case implies negative return, and the bear case is severe. Quality without a margin of safety is not a 12-month buy.
