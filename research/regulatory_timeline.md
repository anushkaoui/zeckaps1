# Regulatory Timeline: Indian Market Infrastructure and F&O

**Cut-off:** 29 October 2026. This timeline covers the rules most relevant to BSE, CDSL, CAMS and KFin. It is not a complete SEBI rulebook.

| Date | Event | Key content | Direct relevance | Status at cut-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Oct 2024 | SEBI circular on equity index derivatives (S06) | Minimum contract size raised to ₹15 lakh; one weekly expiry per exchange; upfront collection of option premium; removal of calendar-spread margin benefit on expiry day; intraday position monitoring; tail-risk coverage | BSE derivatives volumes and yields; CDSL clearing/transaction activity; second-order impact on CAMS/KFin investor activity | Implemented in phases from 20 Nov 2024 to 1 Apr 2025 |
| 29 May 2025 | SEBI index-options position limits (S14) | Staged increase in position limits with a glide path; tighter monitoring of index-option exposures | BSE/NSE derivatives participation; possible mix shift to BSE or NSE depending on limits | Implemented through Jul–Dec 2025; monitor amendments |
| 1 Sep 2025 | SEBI intraday position monitoring framework (S15) | Framework for intraday monitoring of index-derivative positions; additional surveillance measures from Dec 2025 | Compliance cost, volume mix, proprietary/retail participation | Effective Dec 2025 |
| 4 Dec 2025 | SEBI consultation on trading-member position limits (S16) | Proposed changes to position limits for trading members and clients | Potential further cap on derivative volumes and concentration | Consultation; final rules not assumed |
| 20 Aug 2026 | SEBI study on individual traders in equity derivatives (S07) | Documents persistent retail losses; highlights expiry-day concentration and premium-turnover shifts between exchanges | Supports the case for continued regulatory vigilance; not itself a rule | Informational |
| Ongoing | Fee rationalisation in market infrastructure | Competitive pressure on transaction charges, listing fees and RTA fees; SEBI/AMFI scrutiny of total expense ratios and service charges | BSE transaction yields; CAMS/KFin fee pools; CDSL issuer charges | No single binding RTA fee cap identified; risk remains |

## Interpretation

1. **The regime is tightening, not banning.** The 2024–2026 measures raise the cost and reduce the appeal of short-dated options, but they do not eliminate derivatives. BSE's derivatives revenue is therefore exposed to volume, mix and pricing, not to a binary ban.
2. **Expiry-day concentration is the key vulnerability.** SEBI's August 2026 study shows BSE's expiry-day premium ADT rising sharply even as NSE's fell. If regulators target expiry-day concentration further, BSE's fastest-growing revenue line is the most exposed.
3. **Second-order effects matter more for CAMS/KFin than direct effects.** CAMS earns primarily on MF assets, not derivatives. A derivatives downturn hurts CAMS mainly through weaker equity markets, lower investor activity and slower AUM growth.
4. **Fee rationalisation is a permanent risk, not a one-off.** Market-infrastructure fees are politically visible. The revenue-growth assumption implicitly allows for modest fee-yield compression for CAMS/KFin; no explicit RTA fee cap is modelled in the base case.

## What would change the view

- A binding RTA fee cap or mandated common RTA platform: materially negative for CAMS/KFin.
- A further SEBI restriction on weekly expiries or a sharp increase in contract size: negative for BSE, modestly negative for CDSL, second-order negative for CAMS/KFin.
- A formal review of depository charges: negative for CDSL.
- A relaxation of F&O rules: positive for BSE, but not assumed.

**Source caveat:** S14–S16 are cited to SEBI's landing page because the exact circular URLs were not preserved during research. They must be re-verified before external publication.
