# Industry Report: Indian Market Infrastructure, Depositories and MF RTAs

**Cut-off:** 29 October 2026 | **Currency:** ₹ crore unless stated | **Universe:** BSE Ltd, CDSL, CAMS, KFin Technologies

## 1. Executive summary

The four companies sit at different points in the Indian capital-market value chain. BSE is a trading venue and data/listing platform; CDSL is a depository and KYC/transaction utility; CAMS and KFin are mutual-fund registrar and transfer agents (RTAs) with adjacent issuer and international services.

The common characteristics are attractive: regulated licences, high operating leverage, asset-light models, strong cash conversion and structural growth from financialisation of household savings. The differences are decisive for a 12-month investment:

- **CAMS** has the cleanest recurring revenue model, the widest MF RTA share, a 42.2% pre-tax ROCE and a net-cash balance sheet. Its main risk is fee regulation, not volume collapse.
- **BSE** has the highest near-term growth because of index-derivatives share gains, but the highest regulatory and revenue-concentration risk.
- **CDSL** has a genuinely wide moat in retail demat accounts, but its starting valuation leaves little room for slower account growth or fee pressure.
- **KFin** is improving through international and issuer services, but its MF RTA share is smaller and the acquisition/integration profile is more complex.

**Industry conclusion:** the best risk-adjusted position is CAMS. The industry is not a uniform "capital-markets growth" trade; it is a regulatory-fee pool with different sensitivities to equity markets, derivatives volumes and SEBI policy.

## 2. Value chain and revenue pools

```text
Investors / distributors
        │
        ├── Trading venues: BSE, NSE ──► transaction charges, listing, data, co-location
        │            │
        │            └── Clearing corporations: ICCL, CCIL ──► clearing/settlement fees
        │
        ├── Depositories: CDSL, NSDL ──► issuer charges, transaction fees, KYC, corporate actions
        │
        └── Mutual funds: AMCs ◄── RTAs: CAMS, KFin ──► asset-based/folio-based fees
                     │
                     └── Distributors, advisers, investors
```

### 2.1 Exchanges (BSE)

BSE earns from:

- **Transaction charges** — the dominant line. FY26 transaction charges were ₹3,795 crore, about 74% of ₹5,124 crore normalized revenue (BSE's media release shows ₹5,148 crore total revenue, including ₹314 crore investment/other income). In Q1 FY27, transaction charges were ₹1,328 crore, about 85% of ₹1,566 crore revenue, reflecting derivatives volume growth.
- **Listing fees** — recurring and annual, tied to listed companies.
- **Co-location and data** — lower-volatility, high-margin.
- **StAR MF** — mutual-fund transaction platform; Q1 FY27 revenue ₹73.3 crore.
- **Investment income** — on float and investments; Q1 FY27 ₹135 crore.

The economics are exceptionally high-margin: FY26 EBITDA margin was 68%, and Q1 FY27 was 67%. The weakness is concentration: derivatives generate most of the profit, and derivatives are the most politically and regulatorily exposed part of the value chain.

### 2.2 Depositories (CDSL)

CDSL earns from:

- **Annual issuer charges** — recurring fees from listed companies/issuers for maintaining depository records; roughly 40% of revenue.
- **Transaction charges** — settlement and depository transactions; roughly one-fifth of revenue.
- **KYC/AML services** through CVL; a meaningful and growing block.
- **Corporate actions, e-voting, e-locker and other services.**

CDSL's moat is the installed base: 18.59 crore demat accounts at 30 June 2026, about 80% of Indian demat accounts, with AUC of ₹88.2 lakh crore. It added 58 lakh accounts in Q1 FY27 and had 81.4% incremental share in June 2026, although that was down 420 bps from March 2026. The network is two-sided: depository participants and issuers need CDSL, and investors benefit from the widest DP network.

### 2.3 MF RTAs (CAMS and KFin)

RTAs process subscriptions, redemptions, SIPs, folio maintenance, distributor commissions, compliance and investor servicing for AMCs. Revenue is typically asset-based or folio/transaction-based, with annual contracts.

- **CAMS:** FY26 revenue ₹1,516 crore; MF segment 86% and non-MF 14%. Q1 FY27 revenue ₹395 crore, EBITDA margin 46.1%, PAT ₹127.1 crore. It serviced ₹56 lakh crore of MF AUM in Q1 FY27, a 67.2% share; Q4 FY26 share was about 68%. Non-MF revenue grew 28.4% YoY and was 14.9% of revenue.
- **KFin:** FY26 revenue ₹1,301 crore; Q2 FY27 revenue ₹401.8 crore, EBITDA margin 40.1%, PAT ₹100.3 crore. Domestic MF was 58% of Q2 revenue, international & other investor solutions 28% (international ~20%), issuer 13%, with the balance from other services. Its domestic MF AAUM market share was 32.5% in Q2 FY27; the SIP book share was similar.

The RTA market is an oligopoly. Switching costs are high because an AMC's investor records, distributor mappings, compliance history and workflow are embedded in the RTA platform. A switch is operationally risky and requires SEBI/AMC approvals. This supports retention and pricing, but it also makes the RTA a visible cost line when AMCs face TER pressure.

## 3. Structural growth drivers

1. **Financialisation of household savings.** MF AUM reached ₹73.73 lakh crore in FY26; CAMS's serviced AUM was ₹55.1 lakh crore. SIP flows and new investor additions remain the main growth engine.
2. **Retail participation in equities.** Demat accounts have grown rapidly, but the incremental pace is slowing from the post-COVID spike. CDSL's incremental share remains high, yet account additions are cyclical.
3. **Derivatives volume growth.** BSE gained share through index options, particularly expiry-day products. This is the strongest near-term revenue driver but the most fragile.
4. **International and issuer services.** KFin's Ascent acquisition and international investor solutions add a second growth leg; CAMS is growing non-MF services faster than MF.
5. **Digital infrastructure.** KYC, e-voting, data and analytics are adjacent recurring revenue pools, but they also attract regulatory scrutiny and competition.

## 4. Competitive dynamics

| Segment | Structure | Market share evidence | Pricing power | Main competitive threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exchanges | Duopoly (BSE, NSE) | BSE 55.4% notional derivatives share (Apr 2026); ~34% options premium share | Moderate, constrained by exchange competition and SEBI | NSE defending share; regulatory changes |
| Depositories | Duopoly (CDSL, NSDL) | CDSL ~80% of demat accounts; 81.4% incremental share (Jun 2026) | Moderate; issuer charges regulated/visible | NSDL, new technology, fee scrutiny |
| MF RTAs | Oligopoly (CAMS, KFin) | CAMS 67.2% Q1 FY27; KFin 32.5% Q2 FY27 | High but politically visible | Fee caps, AMC in-housing, KFin share gains |
| Issuer/international services | Fragmented/oligopoly | KFin 13% issuer, 28% international/other revenue | Moderate | Global competitors, integration risk |

## 5. Regulatory and fee risk

- **F&O:** The October 2024 SEBI package raised contract sizes, restricted weekly expiries, required upfront premium and tightened intraday monitoring. Later measures increased position limits and monitoring. These are cumulative tightening measures, not a ban. BSE's derivatives revenue is the most exposed; CDSL has a smaller direct exposure; CAMS/KFin face second-order equity-market effects.
- **RTA fees:** SEBI and AMFI periodically review TER and service charges. A binding fee cap or a mandated common RTA platform would compress CAMS/KFin revenue yields. No such cap is in the base case, but it is the key tail risk for CAMS.
- **Depository charges:** CDSL's issuer and transaction charges are visible. A regulatory review could cap or reduce them.
- **Competition policy:** Exchanges' transaction charges and data fees are monitored. BSE's low pricing is a share-gain tool but also a regulatory and competitive risk.

## 7. Industry conclusion

The sector's quality is high, but the market is not paying the same price for each business. BSE offers the highest operating leverage and the highest regulatory tail risk. CDSL offers the widest retail network but is priced for continued double-digit growth. CAMS offers the best combination of recurring revenue, market share, returns and balance-sheet strength. KFin offers a credible improvement story but with more execution and integration risk.

For a 12-month, ₹500 crore mandate, the industry structure supports a **CAMS-first** ranking, with BSE as the high-beta alternative and CDSL/KFin as valuation-dependent alternatives.
