# Adversarial Review and Second Valuation

**Purpose:** challenge the CAMS recommendation, test whether it survives a hostile read, and document what would change the decision.

## 1. The strongest case against CAMS

### 1.1 The target requires a re-rating

At ₹697, CAMS trades at 27.7x FY28E EPS. The ₹800 target is 31.8x FY28E EPS. The base case therefore assumes ~4.1x of multiple expansion, not just earnings growth. If the multiple stays at 27.7x, the stock is worth ₹697 and the 12-month return is only the dividend (~1.8%).

**Rebuttal:** the current 27.7x is below BSE/CDSL/KFin on most cash-flow measures. A 31.8x multiple is still a discount to BSE (42.5x FY28E) and CDSL (49.5x), and is supported by 46% EBITDA margins, 38.7% ROE and net cash. The re-rating is modest, but it is still the weakest link in the thesis.

**Verdict:** the thesis is a "quality at a reasonable price" call, not a deep-value call. Conviction should be moderate, not high.

### 1.2 DCF is only ₹711

The DCF at 10.0% WACC and 6.0% terminal growth is ₹712, only 2.2% above the current price. At an 11.0% WACC/5.0% growth, it is ₹512. The target relies on the relative-valuation leg. If an IC member believes the correct WACC is 11%, the stock is fairly valued to overvalued.

**Rebuttal:** the DCF uses a 4.5% ERP, which is low relative to some India estimates; the resulting 10.0% WACC is valuation-friendly, not conservative. A 5.5–6.0% ERP would lower the DCF to roughly ₹550–590 and the blended target to ~₹760–770. The DCF is a cross-check, not the primary 12-month method; the 75/25 blend deliberately keeps the target below the pure relative value.

**Verdict:** DCF supports "fairly valued", not "cheap". The target is not a DCF target.

### 1.3 Growth guidance may be optimistic

Q1 FY27 revenue grew 11.5%, below the 13% FY27 guidance. The model assumes 13% in FY27 and FY28. If Q2 FY27 does not show acceleration, the FY27 number is at risk.

**Rebuttal:** Q1 PAT grew 17.3%, margins held at 46.1%, and non-MF grew 28.4%. A 13% revenue year is still plausible if AUM growth and non-MF accelerate. The position is staged so Q2 is a gate.

**Verdict:** Q2 is a genuine near-term risk. Do not build the full position before the print.

### 1.4 Fee compression is not explicit

The model grows revenue 13% with no explicit fee-yield cut. If AUM grows 13% but fees compress 2%, revenue grows ~11% and EPS is ~4–5% lower. A binding RTA fee cap would be worse.

**Rebuttal:** CAMS's revenue yield has been broadly stable; the 13% growth assumption is a blend of AUM, non-MF and modest fee pressure. A 2% fee cut would still leave the target near ₹760–780, above the current price.

**Verdict:** fee risk is the main fundamental risk; it is not fully reflected in the base case.

### 1.5 Cash build and capital allocation

The model shows net cash rising to ₹2,161 crore by FY31. That is ~₹87/share of cash, but it assumes no large acquisition and a 65% payout. If CAMS deploys cash at low returns, the ROE and multiple could compress.

**Rebuttal:** net cash is a cushion, not a valuation driver; the DCF adds only the current ₹906 crore. A special dividend or buyback would be accretive. The risk is real but second-order for a 12-month call.

## 2. Second independent valuation

### 2.1 Forward P/E cross-check

| Input | Value |
|---|---:|
| FY28E EPS | ₹25.17 |
| Target P/E | 31.8x |
| Implied target | ₹800 |
| Current FY28 P/E | 27.7x |
| Implied re-rating | +4.1x |

At 30x FY28E, the value is ₹755 (+8.3%); at 32x, ₹806 (+15.6%); at 35x, ₹881 (+26.4%). The ₹800 target is not an aggressive multiple; it is mid-range.

### 2.2 FCF-yield cross-check

| Input | Value |
|---|---:|
| FY27E FCF | ₹558 crore |
| FCF per share | ₹22.48 |
| Target price | ₹800 |
| Implied FCF yield | 2.81% |
| Current FCF yield (FY27E) | 3.23% |

A 2.8% FCF yield for a 13% grower with net cash is reasonable. At a 3.0% yield, the value is ₹749; at 2.5%, ₹899. The target is mid-range.

### 2.3 EV/EBITDA cross-check

| Input | Value |
|---|---:|
| FY28E EBITDA | ₹900 crore |
| Target EV at 21.1x | ₹19,000 crore |
| Add net cash | ₹906 crore |
| Equity value | ₹19,906 crore |
| Per share | ₹800 |

Current EV/FY28E EBITDA is 18.2x. The target assumes 21.1x, still below BSE's 40x and CDSL's 43x FY26 EV/EBITDA. This is a premium to CAMS's current multiple but not an extreme one.

### 2.4 DDM sanity check

A simple Gordon growth check with FY28 DPS of ₹16.36 (65% payout) and cost of equity 10.2% gives ₹651 at 7.5% perpetual dividend growth, ₹803 at 8.0% and ₹1,044 at 8.5%. The DDM is highly sensitive to the perpetual growth assumption and does not independently corroborate the target; it only shows that ₹800 is consistent with ~8.0% perpetual dividend growth.

**Second-path conclusion:** the ₹800 target is corroborated by forward P/E (31.8x), FCF yield (2.8%) and EV/EBITDA (21.1x). It is not corroborated by the DCF (₹712), whose low ERP assumption is itself valuation-friendly. The target is therefore at the upper end of a defensible range; the recommendation should be sized accordingly.

## 3. Hostile read of the rejected names

### BSE

Our FY27E EPS of ₹72.7 may be conservative: Q1 FY27 EPS was ₹21.43, which annualises to ₹85.7. If the Q1 run-rate holds, FY27 EPS could be ₹86–88, and FY28 EPS could be ₹100+. At 42x, that is ₹4,200 (+20%), not ₹3,460. The rejection relies on the regulatory tail, not on weak near-term numbers.

**Response:** fair. BSE's near-term momentum is stronger than our base case. But the bear case is also wider: a 30% derivatives fall plus de-rating takes the stock to ₹1,686. The expected return is still negative because the base case is flat and the bear probability is 25%. Even if an investor assigns only a 10% probability to the bear case and shifts 15 pp to the bull case, BSE's expected target is ~₹3,761 (+7.7% total), still below CAMS's +15.2%. The decision is sensitive to the regulatory probability, not to the growth rate.

### KFin

If international growth stays >30% and margins reach 42%, FY28 EPS could be ₹32–33, and a 40x multiple gives ₹1,280–1,320 (+48–53%). The bull case is real. The rejection relies on the lower quality/margin/share and the integration risk, not on a lack of growth.

**Response:** fair. KFin has the highest bull-case upside. But its bear case (−37.8%) is much deeper than CAMS's (−23.6%), and its base-case return (+10.0%) is lower. A risk-adjusted mandate should prefer CAMS unless KFin's execution risk is clearly resolved.

### CDSL

If demat additions re-accelerate to >40 lakh/quarter and the market keeps a 55x multiple, the stock could reach ₹1,680 (+31%). The rejection relies on the starting multiple and the FY26 profit decline, not on the moat.

**Response:** fair. CDSL is the highest-quality moat in the group. But the base case is negative and the PEG is 5.7x. We would revisit after a 25–30% de-rating or a new growth engine.

## 4. What would make us wrong

1. **CAMS fee cap:** a binding RTA fee cap >10% revenue hit. This would break the thesis; exit trigger 2.
2. **CAMS share loss:** MF RTA share below 63% for two quarters. Exit trigger 1.
3. **Multiple de-rating:** CAMS stays at 27.7x FY28E despite earnings delivery. The target would be ~₹697–750, not ₹800. This is the most likely way to be "right on earnings, wrong on the stock".
4. **BSE regulatory relief:** if SEBI stops tightening and BSE holds >60% share, BSE could out-return CAMS. The ranking would change.
5. **KFin execution:** if margins >42% and international growth >20% for two quarters, KFin's risk-adjusted case improves.

## 5. Conclusion

The CAMS thesis survives the hostile review, but with two caveats:

- **Conviction is moderate, not high.** The DCF is close to the current price; the target relies on a modest re-rating and forward earnings.
- **The position should be built in tranches, not all at once.** The first tranche can be initiated now; Q2 FY27 is a genuine gate for the second tranche. The 4% target (not 5%) reflects the valuation/multiple risk.

The adversarial review does not change the winner. It changes the **sizing and staging**: 4% target, 50/25/25 tranches, and a hard pause above ₹760 before full deployment.
