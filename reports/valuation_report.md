## 2. Relative valuation

**Why 33x?** CAMS deserves a premium to the broad market for 46% EBITDA margins, 38.7% ROE, net cash and a 67% share in an oligopoly. It deserves a discount to BSE/CDSL because its growth is slower and its fees are regulated. 33x FY28E is roughly 1.2x the implied market multiple. At the ₹800 target, the implied FY28 FCF yield is ~3.2%; at 33x it is ~3.1%, which is adequate but not cheap.

## 8. Mandatory shocks

- **F&O −30%:** CAMS has negligible direct derivatives revenue; the modelled revenue impact is −0.6% and EPS −0.7%. The target at the base multiple is broadly unchanged (₹731 vs a FY27 base target of ₹736).
- **Equity −20%:** revenue −13%, EPS −14.3%, FCF ₹478 crore, target at the base multiple ₹631 (−9.5% vs current).
- **Joint adverse:** revenue −13.6%, EPS −15.0%, target ₹532 at 28.05x (−23.7% vs current).

CAMS is the least directly exposed to the F&O shock and the most exposed to the equity drawdown through AUM. That is the correct risk map for an RTA.

## 9. Conclusion

CAMS is worth ₹800 per share on a blended relative/DCF basis, 14.8% above the current price. The DCF alone (₹712) says the stock is fairly valued; the relative case (₹831) says it is modestly undervalued versus its growth and quality. The truth is in between. The recommendation is **BUY** with a 12-month target of ₹800 and a 4% position, with Q2 FY27 confirmation as a gate for the second tranche and the three exit triggers as the sell discipline.
