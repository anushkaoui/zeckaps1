# Scenario and Stress-Test Report

**Cut-off:** 29 October 2026 | **Currency:** ₹ crore | **Prices:** 29 October 2026 close

## 1. Framework

Each company is valued on FY28E EPS and a scenario-specific P/E:

- **Bull (25%):** higher growth/share gains, margin expansion, and a higher exit multiple.
- **Base (50%):** management guidance/our driver forecast and a normalised multiple.
- **Bear (25%):** equity-market drawdown, fee/regulatory pressure, margin compression and a lower multiple.

Probabilities are judgemental but explicit. They sum to 100%. The base case is not a "management case"; it is our central operating forecast.

## 4. Mandatory shock 1: −30% derivatives turnover

- BSE: 70% of revenue exposed to derivatives turnover; 1:1 volume impact; PAT elasticity 1.35.
- CDSL: 10% revenue exposure; PAT elasticity 1.25.

## 7. What would break the CAMS thesis

1. **Fee regulation:** a binding RTA fee cap or common platform that cuts MF revenue yield >10%. Not in the base case; would reduce the target by roughly 15–25%.
2. **Market-share loss:** MF RTA share below 63% for two quarters; would lower growth and the exit multiple.
3. **AUM shock:** a >20% market drawdown with persistent outflows; the equity-drawdown shock already captures a one-year hit.
4. **Execution:** a large, dilutive acquisition or a technology/cyber failure.

## 8. Scenario conclusion

- **CAMS:** best expected return (+15.2%) and shallowest bear case (−23.6%). The base case requires only modest multiple expansion (33x FY28E vs 31.3x FY27E) plus earnings delivery.
- **KFin:** second-best expected return (+10.3%) but a −37.8% bear case and integration risk.
- **BSE:** high bull case (+33% total) but a negative expected return and a −51.9% bear case.
- **CDSL:** negative base/expected return; the quality is already in the price.

## 9. Sources and caveats

S01–S13; model outputs in `outputs/`. Shock elasticities are judgemental and calibrated to disclosed revenue mixes, not econometrically estimated. The equity-drawdown shock is a one-year P&L/AUM impact, not a multi-year path. The joint case is additive, not multiplicative, to avoid double-counting. The bear-case P/E haircuts are judgemental; a higher multiple would reduce the downside.
